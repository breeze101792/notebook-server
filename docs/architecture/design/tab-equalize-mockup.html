Tab-bar width freeze, release & immediate ghost close

Reference mockup for docs/architecture/design/tab-equalization.md and docs/architecture/design/tab-close-animation.md. Hover the tab bar to freeze widths, then click an unpinned tab's × while the pointer is still in the bar. The tab is replaced at once by a ghost whose box collapses and fades over --tab-close-duration; the survivors keep their frozen widths and slide left as its space shrinks. Move the pointer out of the bar and the survivors re-equalize. A close with the pointer outside the bar instead re-equalizes the survivors via nb-tab-equalize. Two pinned tabs sit in the fixed non-scrolling #tab-pinned region and stay put. Tokens and layout are copied from static/css/style.css.

Live tab bar

Ghost close (the shipped motion): with the pointer in the bar, click an unpinned tab's ×. The tab disappears and a same-width ghost takes its slot and collapses (shrink + fade) immediately, over --tab-close-duration --tab-close-ease, while the frozen survivors hold their widths and slide left. The ghost removes itself after TAB_GHOST_FALLBACK_MS; there is no .faded/.leaving class and no transitionend listener. On pointer leave the survivors re-equalize (no reflow plays while a ghost is still on screen). Closing the active tab also ghosts it (the ghost is data, so it survives the re-render; the mockup re-renders synchronously). With the pointer outside the bar the close instead reflows the survivors — the buttons below demonstrate both. The two seeded pinned tabs sit in the fixed, non-scrolling #tab-pinned region at their natural width; they never scroll with the strip and never equalize.
